Publications

CHIMERA-Bench: A Benchmark Dataset for Epitope-Specific Antibody DesignCo-author

M. Ahmed, N. Taj, I. U. Khan, H. Venkateswara, M. Patterson

arXiv:2603.13431 · ICLR 2026 Workshop on Generative and Experimental Perspectives for Biomolecular Design

A unified benchmark for computational antibody design, built around one task: designing CDRs that bind a specified epitope. It fixes the inconsistent datasets, splits and metrics that made existing methods hard to compare.

2,922antibody–antigen complexes
3generalization splits
5metric groups
11methods retrained
Contributions
  • Defined a canonical task, epitope-conditioned CDR sequence–structure co-design, that unifies seven sub-tasks from the literature.
  • Curated a deduplicated, quality-filtered dataset from 20,509 SAbDab entries, with epitope and paratope annotations, CDR masks and contact maps. It is the largest of its kind.
  • Designed epitope-group, antigen-fold and temporal splits with cluster-level leakage prevention, matched on CDR-H3 length, epitope size and antigen size.
  • Built an evaluation protocol covering sequence, structure, binding interface, epitope specificity and designability, including new epitope precision, recall and F1 metrics.
  • Retrained eleven methods across six generative paradigms under identical conditions. Architecture and inductive bias, not model size, drove performance, and interface quality dropped sharply on unseen epitopes.

Deep Learning Approaches for Security Mechanisms in Operating Systems: A ReviewJournal article

A. Masood, N. Taj, Y. A. Shah, J. Arshad

Spectrum of Engineering Sciences, Vol. 4, Issue 1, pp. 95–107. Published 13 January 2026.

A systematic literature review of how deep learning protects operating systems, covering CNN, LSTM and RNN models, federated learning, reinforcement learning and hybrid architectures.

Topics covered
  • Applications in malware detection, anomaly detection, intrusion detection and prevention systems.
  • Comparison of deep learning methods with traditional signature-based approaches.
  • Open challenges: high compute needs, limited and biased datasets, adversarial attacks, cross-platform generalization and model transparency.

ConTact: Contact-First Antibody CDR Design via Explicit Interface ReasoningCo-author

M. Ahmed, S. VonBank, N. Taj, A. Jan, S. Lee, N. Jan, M. Patterson

arXiv:2605.21600

An encoder-decoder that decides where a CDR will touch the antigen before choosing which amino acids to place there, instead of learning both at once.

11–16%better native-contact recovery
3splits, best structural and interface metrics
3stage contact-first decoder
Contributions
  • Identified the conflation of contact identification and sequence prediction as a structural limitation of existing CDR design models, and proposed a contact-first design with an explicit three-stage cascade.
  • Built the cascade from complementarity fingerprinting, supervised contact prediction and contact-gated injection of antigen features.
  • Added distance-biased cross-attention, a contact-weighted cross-entropy loss and a standoff-aware contact-recovery loss that focus learning on binding-critical positions.
  • Beat the baselines on RMSD, native contacts, interface RMSD, DockQ and epitope F1 on all three CHIMERA-Bench splits, while matching the best sequence recovery.
  • Ran diagnostic experiments tracing the sequence-recovery ceiling to backbone geometry at the CDR-H3 apex.
